# Feature: 6a - Part 3 fields

**From build-plan:** feature 6a, the first of two sub-features of item 6 (Form part 3)
**Status:** built on 2026-09-03, awaiting review

## Goal

Build section 3 of the protocol, "Umland, Ufer und Sohle": how the land around the stretch is
used, what the bank looks like, and what the bed is made of. Fifty-four fields, of which
forty-three are percentages that belong to one of six blocks.

Section 3 is a placeholder today. The fields themselves are mostly a repeat of what parts 1 and 2
already built, so the work here is layout and naming rather than new machinery. The one new
control is a percentage box, which appears forty-three times in this section and never again.

**The sum-to-100 rule is 6b, not this sub-feature.** Here every box simply accepts a number and
saves it. Nothing is added up and nothing is checked.

## Why the feature is split

Item 6 reads as one line in the build plan but is the same shape as item 5, which was split for
the same reason: a screenful of fields, and then a rule that turns those fields into something
that behaves.

- **6a, here.** The 54 fields on screen, saving and surviving a reload.
- **6b, next.** The running total under each of the six blocks, and the rule that the total must
  be exactly 100.

Splitting keeps each diff readable. It also keeps the rule honest: the six blocks have to exist
and be named before the thing that adds them up can be written against them.

## Design reference

**There is no mockup for part 3, and none is needed.** Decided on 2026-09-03: the layout does not
have to match the printed form, so long as it reads well and matches the rest of the app. See "No
mockup is needed" at the end of this spec. Two references stand in.

1. **`prototypes/protokoll-teil-1.html` and `prototypes/mockup.css`** for everything that is not
   new: the section card, the `fieldset` and legend, the 12 column grid, the label above the
   control, the hint below it. Part 3 must look like parts 1 and 2 built it.
2. **Page 2 of `Resources/Fiaka_Resources/Formular_Protokoll_E-Befischung_V20260609.pdf`** for the
   arrangement and for every printed label. Section 3 is the top two thirds of that page, from
   "Umland:" down to "Besonderheiten:".

The printed form puts the `%` sign *before* each box and the label after it, four to a line:

```
 % Nadelwald   % Mischwald   % Laubwald   % Auwald
```

We do not copy that. A label above its control is what every field in this app already does, and
what `FeldRahmen` enforces so that the label association cannot be got wrong 43 times. So the
label goes above, the `%` becomes the unit shown beside the box, and the four-across rhythm is
kept with the existing 12 column grid at `spalten={3}`.

## In scope

- One new option list, `ufer.randstreifen`, generated by the existing extraction script from
  labels transcribed off the printed page.
- `FeldProzent`, a percentage box: a number input with a `%` unit, built on the existing
  `FeldText` rather than beside it.
- The **Umland** block: 8 percentages.
- The **Ufer** block: the Randstreifen radio, the 4 Neigung percentages, the geschütteter Damm
  pair, the Wurzeln percentage, the 9 Uferbewuchs percentages and the 8 Uferverbauung
  percentages, plus the Buhnenbereich checkbox and the two "sonstige" free-text boxes.
- The **Gewässersohle** block: the 8 Substrat percentages, the 6 Sohlverbauung percentages and
  the 4 Besonderheiten checkboxes.
- `Antworten` grows `umland`, `ufer` and `gewaessersohle`.
- The six percentage blocks defined once as data, in `abschnitte/teil3/bloecke.ts`, and rendered by
  mapping over that definition. See "The blocks are data" below.
- German labels in `frontend/src/i18n/locales/de.json`.
- A test that every path in those block definitions is a real field path in `felder.json`.

## Out of scope

- **The sum-to-100 rule and the running totals.** All of 6b. Until then a block may total 43 or
  250 and the form says nothing.
- **The legacy form's two defects in this section.** [Defect 1](../../docs/ffs-defect-list.md),
  the substrate block that is never checked at submit, and [defect 4](../../docs/ffs-defect-list.md),
  the `Lehm / Ton` box that throws instead of warning. Both are defects *in the checking*, so both
  are 6b's to fix, and 6b must state that it has.
- **Rejecting a keystroke.** The legacy form refuses the value that would push a block over 100.
  We will not, and 6b carries the decision and the reason.
- **Checking a single box against 0 and 100.** Step 2 puts `min`, `max` and `step` on the number
  input, which bounds the spinner and tells a browser what kind of value this is. That is an
  affordance, not a rule: nothing in 6a rejects a typed `250` or `-5`, and no message appears. The
  rule that catches it is 6b's, alongside the block total.
- **The `#` marker and its legend.** The printed form marks each of the six blocks with a `#` and
  prints "= Summe muss 100 betragen!" at the foot of the page. That marker is the sum rule
  announcing itself, so it arrives with the rule in 6b, not here.
- **Required-field enforcement.** Unchanged from parts 1 and 2: an asterisk marks what feature 11
  will demand at submit, and nothing is blocked here. The percentage boxes get no asterisk at all,
  because what is required is a block totalling 100, not any one box. 6b marks the blocks.
- **The rest of page 2.** "Natürliche Strukturen im Wasser", "Nutzungsbedingte Einflüsse" and
  "Fischereiliche Bewirtschaftung" are printed on the same page but belong to section 4, feature 7.
- **The `check_ok_*` and `check_n_*` fields.** Thirteen PDF fields that are the legacy form's own
  red-star and green-tick indicators. They are not answers, they get no key in the document, and
  what they indicate is 6b's running total.
- **The backend half.** No endpoint, no Pydantic model, for the same reason as parts 1 and 2:
  features 2 and 3 are deferred.

## Section 3 does not vary with the Gewässertyp

Worth saying outright, because 5b has just finished teaching the opposite. Hydrology disappears on
a standing water. Nothing in section 3 does.

A lake has an Umland, a bank and a bed, and the legacy form has no handler anywhere in section 3
that reads the Gewässertyp. Confirmed against `all_js_Formular.js` on 2026-09-03. So there is no
suppression to build here and no "does not apply" value to write, and 6b's rule applies to all
eight water types equally.

## Build loop

Build one step at a time, never the whole feature at once.

1. Plan mode lays out the step before any code.
2. The AI implements just that step.
3. It shows the diff (not full files); you read it and understand it.
4. You approve, then choose whether to commit a checkpoint or roll straight on.
   Checkpoints are optional; `/complete` makes the real feature-level commit at the end.

Never accept a step you haven't read. If a diff is too big to review, the step was too big, so split it.

## The blocks are data

Parts 1 and 2 write every field out by hand in its block component, and at six to fifteen
heterogeneous fields that is the right shape. Part 3 is not that. It is six runs of between four
and nine controls that are identical apart from a path and a label.

So the six blocks are declared once, in `abschnitte/teil3/bloecke.ts`, as an ordered list of
`{ pfad, labelKey }` per block, and each block component maps over its own definition. Three
reasons, in order of weight:

1. **6b needs exactly this list.** A running total is the sum over a block's paths. Written as data
   here, 6b reads it; written as JSX here, 6b would have to retype all 43 paths and the two copies
   would drift.
2. **It makes the path test possible.** `Antworten` is a TypeScript interface and vanishes at
   build time, so nothing can enumerate it at runtime. A declared array can be checked against
   `felder.json`, which is already in the repository and already aliased into the frontend build as
   `@formular`. Typing the array as `AntwortPfad[]` also makes a path that is not in the answers
   document a build error, so the two checks together close both directions.
3. **Forty-three near-identical JSX elements is the repetition the standards already rule out.**

**Only the percentage runs.** The Randstreifen radio, the geschütteter Damm pair, the Buhnenbereich
and Besonderheiten checkboxes, the Wurzeln box and the two free-text boxes stay written out in the
component like every other field in the app. They differ from each other, so a shared definition
would buy nothing and cost readability.

### Why the path test earns its place

Every one of these names has to match the legacy PDF path exactly, because
`coding-standards.md` makes that match the thing that keeps the eventual FiaKa transfer a direct
mapping rather than a lookup table.

A misspelling is invisible in the app: the field renders, accepts input and saves. It surfaces
years later as a column FiaKa cannot fill. Some near-misses this section actually contains:

| Looks like | Is actually |
|---|---|
| `ufer.neigung` | not the four-way Neigung block, but the single slope in degrees of the geschütteter Damm |
| `ufer.drahtnetze` | the bank, whereas the bed is `gewaessersohle.drahtnetze_sohlverbauung` |
| `ufer.uferverbau_keiner` | the bank, whereas the bed is `gewaessersohle.keine_sohlverbauung` |

### The two "sonstige" pairs

The printed form ends both the Uferbewuchs and the Uferverbauung rows with a `%` box whose label

Do not render the text box with a bare "Sonstiges" label. Two fields with that label sit within
three rows of each other and would be indistinguishable to a screen reader, which is the same
problem 5a solved for the two Schätzwert boxes. Label each for the list it belongs to.

## Testing

`npm test` (vitest, from `frontend/`) and `pytest` (from `backend/`) are both on, so the gate in
`coding-standards.md` applies.

**In-scope logic needing a test:**

- **Step 1** rides on the extraction script's existing pairing guard and its existing test file. A
  new label map does not need a new test; it needs the existing guard to still raise, which the
  step's done-when covers.
- **Step 6** adds the path and count test over `bloecke.ts`, which is the only genuinely new logic
  in this sub-feature.

Steps 2 to 5 are field layout. `coding-standards.md` says components and integration surfaces are
verified with browser evidence and the build, not with unit tests, so they ride on screenshots,
a reload check and `npm run build`. That is the same call parts 1 and 2 made.

Note that `bloecke.ts` is data, not logic, so the test is not asserting behaviour. It is asserting
that 43 hand-transcribed strings match the source of truth, which is the one thing in this
sub-feature that can be wrong without anything looking wrong.

**Manual path**, at each step: start the frontend dev server, open a draft, go to section 3, type
into the new fields, reload, confirm the values came back. Check light and dark, and desktop width
and below 800px. Tab through the block and confirm the order is the reading order and that nothing
traps focus.

## Notes for the AI

- **Every label comes off page 2 of the PDF, not from the field name.** `umland.wiese` is printed
  "Wiese / Weide", `gewaessersohle.lehm` is "Lehm / Ton", `ufer.mauer_unverfugt` is
  "Mauer/Pflaster, unverfugt". Extracted text of that page is the reference; do not shorten a label
  to match a key.
- **Keep the grain sizes and the degree ranges.** "Sand (<2 mm)", "Kies (>2 mm)", "Flachufer,
  0-20°". They are the question, not decoration.
- **Correct an obvious typo in a display label, never an export value.** The standing rule from
  4b and 5a, recorded in the extraction script's comments.
- **No `pflicht` on a percentage box.** What is required is a block totalling 100. Marking 43
  individual boxes with an asterisk would say something false.
- **`FeldProzent` wraps `FeldText`, it does not copy it.** One control, one place where the label
  association and the aria wiring live. `coding-standards.md` and the MUI rule both point the same
  way.
- **No new colour and no `sx` override at a call site.** Tokens in `theme.css`, configured once in
  `muiTheme.ts`.
- **Accessibility is an acceptance criterion, not a later pass.** Label above every control,
  visible focus, contrast in both themes, and a sub-heading structure inside the Ufer and
  Gewässersohle blocks that a screen reader can navigate. Three `fieldset`s with five sub-headings
  is a real structure question, not just styling: prefer nested `fieldset`/`legend` over a bare
  heading if the block reads as a group of groups.
- **Untouched is not wrong, as everywhere else.** A blank box means nobody has been in it, and a
  blank box is never a message. `istLeer` in `regeln/regel.ts` is where that convention lives, and
  6b's rule follows it: a block where every box is blank raises nothing.
- **Do not add up anything.** If a running total starts to feel natural while laying out a block,
  that is 6b arriving early. Leave it.

## Amended during the build

Recorded here rather than left as silent divergences from the draft, following the precedent 5a
set. All five came out of the two review passes on 2026-09-03.

1. **`ProzentBlock` was extracted.** The draft had each block component write out its own
   `fieldset`, `legend`, `grid` and `.map`, which came to the same five-line shape five times over.
   Since 6b adds a running total and a required marker to every one of those, the repetition would
   have made that five edits across two files. The five runs now share one component, so 6b changes
   one file. It still adds nothing up.
2. **`NEIGUNG` was renamed `UFERNEIGUNG`.** It holds the four bank-steepness shares, while
   `ufer.neigung` is the Damm's angle in degrees. The collision was costing three separate
   explanatory comments; the name now carries what the comments were saying, and it matches its
   siblings `UFERBEWUCHS` and `UFERVERBAUUNG`.
3. **`FeldHaken` gained an optional `spalten`.** The Buhnenbereich checkbox shares a grid row with
   two labelled fields, and the draft solved its alignment with a hand-built wrapper div at the
   call site. Parts 4 to 6 have more checkboxes in more rows, so the placement lives in the control
   instead, where it gets solved once.
4. **The Randstreifen radio lost its required marker.** The draft marked it `pflicht` by analogy
   with part 2's radio groups. Checked against `validation.js`: the legacy form requires the
   Gewässertyp, the three Messdaten groups, the nine hydrology groups and five of the six
   percentage blocks, and never the Randstreifen. An asterisk would have promised a gate feature 11
   has no grounds to build.
5. **Three labels are deliberately not verbatim**, and the reasoning is the spec's own
   anti-ambiguity rule rather than an oversight:

   | Printed on page 2 | Ours | Why |
   |---|---|---|
   | `ohne` | `ohne Bewuchs` | "ohne" above a box says nothing on its own |
   | `Neigung ca.` | `Neigung des Damms, ca.` | otherwise a second field called Neigung, three rows under the first |
   | `Gewässersohle, Substrate:` | `Gewässersohle` | the section holds Sohlverbauung and Besonderheiten too, so naming it for the substrates alone would be wrong |

   Everything else is verbatim, including `Mauer/Pflaster, unverfugt`, which was briefly respaced
   to `Mauer / Pflaster` during the build and put back.

## No mockup is needed, decided 2026-09-03

The draft raised the missing part 3 mockup as an open question. It is settled: **the layout does
not have to match the printed PDF, and no mockup will be made.** Whatever arrangement reads well
for these fields is acceptable.

What the PDF is still authoritative for does not change:

| From the PDF | Ours to decide |
|---|---|
| Which fields exist, and their exact legacy paths | How they are arranged on screen |
| The printed label on each one | How many sit in a row |
| Which fields form a block, and the sum-to-100 rule | How a block is visually grouped and headed |
| Export values, never altered | Spacing, order within a row, where a unit sits |

The constraint that remains is internal consistency: part 3 should look like parts 1 and 2 built
it, using the same card, legend, grid and label-above-control that `FeldRahmen` already enforces.
That is a stronger target than the PDF anyway, since a surveyor moves between these six sections
in one sitting.
